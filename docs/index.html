Drills & Training Resources

Practice these on your own between sessions. Repetition is where improvement lives.

⚽

Cone Dribbling Slalom

10 min · Solo · Ball control

Set up 8 cones in a straight line, 1 yard apart. Dribble through with your dominant foot, then weak foot. Focus on tight touches and keeping your head up.

Watch Demo →
🎯

Wall Passing — 1-Touch

15 min · Solo · First touch

Find a solid wall and practice one-touch passing at varying distances. Alternate feet. Start slow and build speed. 100 touches minimum per session.

Watch Demo →
💪

Lateral Band Walk

10 min · Solo · Strength

Use a resistance band above the knees. Stay low, take lateral steps for 20 yards each direction. Builds hip and glute strength critical for change-of-direction speed.

Watch Demo →
🏃

Sprint & Recover Intervals

20 min · Solo · Fitness

Sprint full field length, walk back. Repeat 8 times. Rest 2 min. Do 2 sets. Mirrors the high-intensity demands of a real match and builds match fitness fast.

Watch Demo →
🦵

Shooting on Target

20 min · Partner or Solo · Finishing

Place the ball 18 yards from goal. Aim for corners. 10 shots each foot. Add a dribble before the shot for added difficulty. Track how many you hit on frame.

Watch Demo →
🧠

Pressure Decisions Drill

15 min · 3+ players · Game IQ

2v1 in a small grid. The defender applies pressure while the two attackers must pass and move. Decision speed is the goal — not just technique.

Fuel Your Training

Nutrition & Recovery

What you eat and how you recover is half the work. These guidelines keep you performing at your best.

Pre-Training

Before You Train

  • Eat a balanced meal 2–3 hours before: rice, pasta, or oats with lean protein.
  • Have a small snack 30–60 min before if needed — banana or toast with peanut butter.
  • Hydrate throughout the day. Aim for at least 2 liters before a session.
  • Avoid high-fat, high-fiber foods right before training — they slow you down.
Quick Pre-Game Meal Pasta with chicken and a light tomato sauce, eaten 2.5 hrs before. Simple, effective, trusted by pros.
Post-Training

After You Train

  • Eat within 30–60 minutes of finishing — the "recovery window" is real.
  • Prioritize protein to repair muscle: eggs, chicken, Greek yogurt, or a protein shake.
  • Rehydrate with water and consider electrolytes after intense sessions.
  • Sleep 8–9 hours — most muscle repair happens while you sleep.
Recovery Snack Idea Greek yogurt + granola + berries. Hits protein, carbs, and antioxidants in one easy meal.

Strength Training

Strength Training Plan

3 workouts per week focusing on lower body strength and core stability — the foundation of a powerful, injury-resistant player.

This progressive plan runs June through August. Each phase builds on the last: we start with bodyweight foundations, then add load and power as tryouts approach. Rest days matter — don't skip them. If a session falls on a technical training day, do the strength work after a 4-hour gap or the following morning.

🦵
Lower Body StrengthSquats, lunges, single-leg work — for speed, shooting power, and stability
🧱
Core StabilityPlanks, rotational work, anti-rotation — for balance, tackling, and ball striking
⚡
Power & ExplosivenessPlyometrics introduced in Phase 2 — for first-step quickness and jumping
Rest Between Sets60–90 sec for strength sets; 2 min for heavier lifts in Phase 3
Warm-UpAlways 5–8 min: leg swings, hip circles, glute bridges, and a light jog
ProgressionAdd weight or reps each week only if last session felt controlled throughout
Form FirstNever sacrifice form for load. A clean bodyweight squat beats a sloppy weighted one.
